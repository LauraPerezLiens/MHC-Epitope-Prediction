# NAP-CNB scientific and technical history

Repository evidence reviewed on 20 November 2026.

## 1. Scientific objective and interpretation of the target

NAP-CNB was developed as a research pipeline for epitope classification using protein language-model features and predicted secondary structure. The repository identifies the work as part of doctoral research in computational biology and cancer immunotherapy at CNB-CSIC. Its implemented prediction unit is a 25-residue window of a parent protein. The historical family-level positive target indicates that a window spans an epitope associated with qualifying positive evidence in the relevant MHC context. Other eligible windows may enter the binary dataset as operational negatives. A 25-residue window is the computational context around evidence, which can describe a shorter peptide.

Class I and class II MHC molecules provide distinct contexts for peptide presentation to T cells. An MHC binding assay concerns peptide–MHC binding, whereas identification of an eluted ligand provides evidence about presentation under the assayed conditions. A T-cell response assay concerns recognition or a measured cellular response. These observations support different biological inferences. NAP-CNB combines qualifying evidence from MHC and T-cell export families to define a window-level classification label. The target therefore concerns recorded epitope evidence under the dataset rules. It does not quantify binding affinity, antigen presentation probability, or immunogenicity as separate outcomes. Operational negatives carry no blanket claim of experimentally verified absence of those properties.

## 3. Original pipeline

### 3.1 Assays, reference sequences and observation identity

Historical acquisition combined positive MHC and T-cell IEDB exports for eleven families. Human class I included HLA-A/B/C and class II included HLA-DP/DQ/DR. The mouse strata used H2-D/K/L and H2-IA/IE. The family and species fields describe the assay grouping used by the construction, and the parent FASTA reference describes the antigen source. A family group pools restrictions at the resolution represented in the exports and classification code.

The historical downloader merged assay records using peptide text, coordinates and source/parent identifiers. The classifier downloaded parent protein sequences, mapped peptide evidence to the parent, and enumerated length-25 windows. A matched interval could be contained in several overlapping windows. Windows without an assigned positive interval entered the operational negative category. For a window with more than one candidate epitope, the historical output stored a selected epitope and a position score. This compact row format helped downstream training and left less assay-level detail available for later review. The reconstruction preserves the full eligible event relation instead.

The historical classifier assigned `protein_group_id` to each distinct protein URL within a classification file. It assigned `group_id` by original window row in that file. The numerical IDs restart between files. Historical derivative rows preserve the local window group. Reordering could add recorded source offsets to the group number. External joins therefore need the source filename or manifest entry, rather than a bare integer. A primary sequence shared across families, proteins or coordinates may represent distinct observations. A hash of the complete protein additionally identifies exact protein-sequence aliases, which neither local integer establishes on its own.

### 3.2 Structure prediction and feature storage

The TensorFlow/Keras **UNet-C Ensemble**, also called ProteinUnet in older documentation, predicts a three-state structural sequence C/H/E from a complete parent protein. The implementation processes proteins in padded segments of up to 1,024 residues. For longer proteins it uses overlapping segments, discards internal margins where possible, averages retained probabilities and assigns the highest-probability state per residue. The structural mapper checks coordinates and exact window-to-parent agreement before extracting 25 states. The available SavedModel and variable files belong to one local checkpoint. Archived predictions are certified through their own matching and provenance checks.

**SecondaryBERT** is a separate encoder. It was trained with masked-token modeling on predicted structures from a UniRef-derived corpus. Its recorded validation subset contained 100,000 proteins and gave masked-token accuracy 0.885621 and C/H/E macro-F1 0.873589. These values measure reconstruction of the structural alphabet. For feature generation, the encoder averages the final states at the 25 biological positions to produce 768 values. **ProtT5** supplies 1,024 sequence features. The historical generator uses a Rostlab encoder, token-level attention-mask pooling and an output array of float32 values. Its code includes a documented tokenizer and special-token policy, while the exact runtime configuration of archived batches remains partially unresolved.

Historical augmentation used BLOSUM62-based sequence derivatives with the parent group's label and structural representation. The primary features were stored for the input sequence, and the per-group structural features were reused. The resulting joint input has 1,792 columns. The historical tree stored NPY matrices, row and group metadata, and compressed source files with receipts. It contained 1,449,852,950 augmented and original rows, initially in 14,503 source blocks and later in 14,499 globally reordered chunks. These block counts refer to different physical layouts of the same reported row universe. The archived corpus and current D1 represent different evidence and acquisition contracts. A synthetic sequence assigned the parent's label is a modeling assumption and adds no observed assay outcome.

### 3.3 Historical models, scaling and evaluation

The reference MLP takes 1,792 inputs, two hidden maps of width 512 and 128, and one output logit. The documented LayerNorm reference uses ReLU, dropout 0.30, BCEWithLogitsLoss and AdamW at learning rate 0.001 with weight decay 0.0001. Cosine warm restarts used a 20-epoch initial period and a minimum learning rate of 0.00001 in the referenced ladder. The common TRAIN protocol used batches of 8,192 with 4,096 rows of each class, seed 42 and one GPU per run. This is class resampling. The reference 100-epoch ladder did not implement early stopping. Later launchers and runs changed stage lengths, scaling and evaluation cadence. Early BatchNorm models had a distinct inference-state contract.

Initial scaling used TRAIN rows from two physical chunks. A ten-chunk comparison was prepared, and the available record lacks enough retained results for a quantitative conclusion. A later scaler used 1,159,882,361 TRAIN rows across the 14,503-block layout. The globally shuffled layout had its own recorded all-chunk TRAIN scaler and manifest. Training shuffled samples, and an experimental change shuffled chunk visitation between epochs. Global row reordering before split assignment was a separate transformation. The historical splitter stratified rows approximately 80/10/10 by label within each mixed chunk and permitted related observations to cross the boundaries.

STAGE measured fixed balanced samples from the current chunk scope. ALL measured fixed balanced samples spanning the complete chunk pool. ALL was a sampling design, so its metrics should be read as outcomes on that sample. The documented MLP used TEST STAGE loss for checkpoint selection. This made TEST a selection population. “TRAIN ONLINE” accumulated metrics while weights changed and is distinct from a fixed-weight validation or test evaluation. The logistic regression code implemented both a natural-prevalence run and a later exact-50/50 protocol. The natural run used one linear logit, Adam with a separate L2 term and the recorded TRAIN scaler. Its configuration specifies batches of 8,192, learning rate 0.001, L2 0.0001, seed 42 and a ten-epoch target. The balanced run used one linear logit with the MLP sampling and checkpoint protocol, AdamW weight decay and fixed balanced evaluation samples. These experiments expose different training prevalences and have different metric records.

## 4. Why the project moved to v2

### 4.1 Evaluation and dependence

The group audit measured 55,595,967 of 55,763,575 scoped historical groups in multiple splits, with 48,726,754 in all three. This establishes fragmentation of window groups and their derivatives under the historical row split. The group audit did not measure exact primary overlap or quantify the causal effect of overlap on individual AUC values. The same-checkpoint STAGE/ALL comparison demonstrates sensitivity to evaluation scope, and the BatchNorm comparison demonstrates sensitivity to inference statistics. Changing prevalence, chunk composition, training history and selection on TEST also complicates attribution. A high score on a local row-based sample alone cannot determine how a model behaves on a protein-independent cohort.

The practical response in v2 was to establish a shared split by exact primary identity with an explicit provenance check for historical groups. This removes exact primary and exact representation overlap. Protein-level and coordinate overlap remain separately measured limitations. The resulting design gives an evaluation estimand that can be stated and checked.

### 4.2 Evidence semantics and conflicting labels

The historical contradiction audit found 3,494,284 distinct primaries assigned both labels among 15,135,904 unique sequences from 55,763,575 original rows. A model without family or protein identity in its features can encounter different labels for closely related or identical observations. The contradiction audit groups by primary and explicitly excludes an embedding comparison. Different structures can distinguish some rows, and context-specific labels can be biologically meaningful. Neither fact eliminates the observed ambiguity for exact representations carrying both labels.

Source audits then examined whether changes in normalization and coordinate handling would improve traceability. The A/B replay processed 1,985,260 records in 22 exports. A reproduced 948,344 events from historical merged keys. B retained the assays in 1,016,016 groups with richer identity and explicit unresolved states. The modified-peptide audit separated a modified entity from an unmodified sequence counterpart. Evidence for the modified entity and evidence for a simple peptide could be linked without conflating them. The pending-evidence and coordinate audits distinguished exact mapping, verified projection and unresolved reference cases. The counts in these reports describe distinct units, such as raw assays, grouped events and affected windows. They cannot be summed as though they measured disjoint windows.

The v2 decision was to reconstruct evidence and labels from validated records and trace, rather than edit disputed historical rows in place. The reconstruction preserves family-specific labels and adds global evidence aggregation as a separate dataset view. This supports two different scientific questions with one provenance record.

### 4.3 Representation and identity

An earlier modality sample traced 33 groups across eleven sources with zero failures. Its scope did not cover every feature row or establish pre-shuffle primary provenance. The later embedding audit found exact historical primary candidates for 14,260,167 input keys, but a matching key alone did not establish a compatible vector. Extended numerical testing reported four failures under its criteria. Two improved under the FP32 diagnostic, while two worsened. Correct addressing was verified for those cases, and the cause remained undetermined. Batch chronology and archive receipts showed candidate series but certified no common execution configuration. These findings supported full regeneration under a pinned protocol for ProtT5.

For SecondaryBERT, evidence was stronger. The canonical sources shared a documented mapping, dimension, numerical agreement across repeated candidates and configuration fingerprint. Older `chunks_pooled/` payloads differed from canonical `chunks/` and were kept outside certified reuse. A controlled reference sample then established numerical agreement with the local checkpoint. This supported a different implementation choice, namely preserving certified historical addresses and generating only missing keys.

## 5. NAP-CNB v2 releases and validation

### 5.1 Verified evidence and definitive structural selection

Phase 1 retained eligible simple positive evidence when the epitope interval was verifiable against the parent reference. Pending evidence remained traceable, and windows withheld by the reconstruction remained outside the binary dataset. The binary stage contained 49,205,104 windows, including 5,594,529 positives. The selected release requires verified 25-state structure in addition to the binary label. It uses 48,654,944 base structures and 468,559 verified recoveries. The complement excludes 81,601 windows, of which 7,455 are positive and 74,146 operational negative. The definitive totals are **49,123,503 windows, 5,587,074 positives and 43,536,429 operational negatives**.

The reconstruction uses family-specific Parquet, a recovery overlay and exclusions keyed by `window_id`. The release signs the relationship rather than storing a separate full copy. Protein hashes, coordinates, assay and epitope evidence remain traceable. Structural availability is a selection condition. Its impact varies across families, including 46 removed positives among 145 binary positives in H2-L. This attrition is relevant to comparisons of family prevalence.

### 5.2 Certified features

The ProtT5 full run produced **14,530,324** exact-primary vectors of 1,024 float32 values. Revision `94a6abc029ae13029317b140b7424e012bf8dfbf` and local model/tokenizer assets were pinned. RTX 3090 physical GPU 2 processed batches of 256 with FP16 inference and FP32 averaging. The store contains 146 NPY shards and 56,814 confirmed SQLite batches. Generation with internal checking took 7.054 hours and the full recorded operation took 7.116 hours. The reported mean generation rate was 572.17 sequences/s. The 59.523 GB store was reopened and verified across the committed ranges, including shape, dtype, finiteness and digests binding keys to bytes. Reopening made zero new inferences.

SecondaryBERT covers **1,847,436** exact C/H/E inputs with 768 float32 values. The index references **1,819,032** historical vectors and holds **28,404** newly produced vectors, including the 1,000-row pilot and 27,404 final rows. A bounded comparison of 264 historical vectors from eleven canonical sources showed exact numerical agreement. Full archival checks and the comparison serve different evidentiary roles. The final SQLite file contains the references and new vectors in 416,288,768 bytes. Historical access still incurs Zstd decompression and receipt checking, with a measured median of 4.73 seconds per sampled block. That latency is an access-planning constraint, independent of coverage.

### 5.3 Exact deduplication and label views

The base consolidates rows only when conservative protein identity, MHC family, exact primary, exact secondary and original label all match. Primary and secondary keys already identify their certified vectors, making a vector-by-vector equality check unnecessary for this operation. Distinct URLs survive even when their protein hashes match. The representative uses the smallest lexicographic `window_id`. A reversible relation associates every canonical window with its representative and retains access to the original coordinates and assay evidence. This consolidated **101,143** exact duplicates, leaving **49,022,360** base rows, **5,587,073** positives and **43,435,287** operational negatives.

The global index applies `MAX(label_original)` over all base observations of each exact primary. This produces **14,530,324** keys, **3,577,364** positive and **10,952,960** operational negative. A global positive signifies qualifying evidence in at least one observed context. The base preserves family-specific labels for family-specific evaluation. The global dataset has one row per distinct primary and secondary, preserving the **547,627** primaries associated with multiple secondary structures. It contains **15,100,592** representations, **3,844,628** positive and **11,255,964** operational negative. The documented 3,378,126 primary/secondary pairs with both original labels precede global consolidation and help explain why the two views estimate different targets.

### 5.4 Shared splits and D1

Version 4C fixes one split per exact primary using seed **42** and deterministic key hashing. It checked 51,593,373 historical identifiers scoped by source file. Each checked identifier links to a single canonical window. The 1,675,495 windows with multiple candidate identifiers retain those relationships as candidates, and 1,563,062 windows have no recovered link. None of the observed scoped links creates a cross-primary edge. This supports one effective group per exact primary for this release. The reopened manifest reproduced all assignments and the base and global views inherit the same partitions.

The independence constraint covers exact primary sequences and exact primary/secondary pairs. **28,475 of 28,485 full-protein sequence hashes** occur in multiple splits. An interval check found **49,118,918** original windows overlapping a window from another split. The resulting evaluation represents generalization to held-out exact primaries inside a substantially shared protein universe. It does not establish generalization to unseen proteins.

D1 is the validated original-only global manifest. It binds global rows, exact-key feature stores and seed-42 partitions with hashes and coverage counts. It requires no copy of either embedding modality.

## 6. Critical limitations and next experiments

**Labels and coverage.** Operational negatives can include undiscovered positives, and evidence density is uneven across proteins, assays, families and species. The global rule collapses known context dependence into an evidence union. It is suitable for a global evidence-classification benchmark with its own estimand. Family-specific claims require the family labels, evidence strata and reporting at an appropriate restriction resolution. Changes in data acquisition, coordinate validation and structural filtering can shift prevalence. Rare subgroups need counts and uncertainty alongside point estimates. H2-L contributes four validation and eight test positives in the current split.

**Dependence and comparisons.** Shared proteins and neighboring windows make ordinary row-level uncertainty intervals optimistic when errors are correlated. Report the exact-primary evaluation and separately propose a protein-held-out evaluation. A future comparison should specify how to handle identical full proteins under different accessions and audit shared primary keys and overlapping or highly related windows before interpreting protein-level generalization. Distinct assays and endpoints should be compared only within their appropriate evidence and population. An external ligand-binding predictor and this window classifier may have different targets and input units, so direct numerical comparisons require an explicitly matched task.

**Reproducibility.** Historical outputs, cached feature representations and checkpoint tables provide evidence at different levels. Some deleted training artifacts prevent replay of the exact model. Experiment timestamps and local file metadata are insufficient to settle undocumented hyperparameters. Future reports can pin selection, split and embedding hashes, software versions, scaler provenance and checkpoint-selection rules. The I/O cost of the historical secondary shards warrants measurement under the actual read pattern before considering repacking. Results on D1 have yet to be obtained.

**Proposed experimental sequence.** Use D1 to compare logistic regression and MLP as pre-specified baselines under the same input manifest and holdout. Fit preprocessing on TRAIN and select hyperparameters and thresholds using VALIDATION. Evaluate an untouched TEST once per finalized configuration. Compare unweighted and balanced training protocols with common evaluation prevalence. Report ROC-AUC, precision–recall performance with its positive prevalence, thresholded precision/recall/F1 and confusion matrices. Add calibration assessment where predicted scores support interpretation as probabilities. Stratify errors by family and available evidence type through the base provenance, and keep global and family-specific targets distinct. Evaluate a separate protein-held-out protocol before drawing conclusions about novel proteins. These are proposed analyses. D1 and the metadata contracts support preparation, while model training, calibration, external comparisons and D2 results remain outside the completed record.
